Case study

Renewable Commercial
Mandate

Defining end-to-end commercial models and portfolio management capabilities for a major European utility building its renewable pipeline.

Context

European utilities are under mounting pressure to accelerate the build-out of renewable capacity while maintaining commercial discipline and alignment with risk policy. This mandate arose when a major utility, with a well-established project pipeline, discovered that its engineering, commercialisation and operations functions held fundamentally different views on how that build-out should be governed — and who was accountable for what.

Without a shared commercial framework, the organisation risked misaligned investment decisions, unhedged exposures, and a target operating model that no single function could own.

"Without a shared commercial framework, the organisation risked misaligned investment decisions and a target operating model that no function could fully own."

Crescenti Pohl Advisory · Project observation

Client situation

The starting point
  • Target renewables portfolio under development across core European markets
  • Established pipeline of renewable projects across generation technologies
  • Commercialisation and operations required to align with group risk policy
The core challenge
  • Fundamentally different understanding between engineering, commercial and operations functions on how targets should be achieved
  • Unclear requirements, accountability gaps and no agreed path to a concrete target operating model
  • Risk of misaligned commercial decisions across the full asset lifecycle

Our approach

We structured our engagement in four sequential phases, moving from stakeholder alignment through to capability review and model validation — ensuring each step built organisational consensus before proceeding.

Stakeholder alignment & mandate scoping
Commercial model definition per asset type
E2E capability mapping by commercial model
Trading capability review in core markets

Our contribution

Commercial mandate across the full renewable function

Took full commercial responsibility covering project development, asset commercialisation and the operation of renewables contracts — bridging the gap between engineering and trading functions.

Commercial model definition aligned to risk appetite

Developed and documented commercial models for each asset category — merchant, fully-hedged, PPA-backed, and hybrid — each calibrated to the client's group risk policy and market context.

End-to-end capability mapping per commercial model

Produced a structured map of required portfolio management and trading capabilities for each commercial model — from origination and pricing through to settlement and reporting.

Trading capability review across core markets

Assessed existing trading capabilities market by market, identifying gaps and prioritising build-vs-buy decisions to reach the required capability level for each commercial model.

Outcome

The engagement delivered a concrete, jointly owned target operating model for the renewable function — with clear commercial accountability across project development, asset operation, and portfolio management. The capability gap analysis provided the basis for a structured roadmap of investments in trading infrastructure, people, and processes across the client's core markets.

By establishing a common commercial language across functions, the client was able to proceed with its renewable pipeline with substantially reduced coordination risk and a clear governance structure aligned to group risk policy.
