Context

Hydro pump storage assets occupy a unique strategic position in the European energy system. They provide both storage and flexible generation capacity, with value derived from multiple market vectors: energy arbitrage, ancillary services, and capacity payments. This complexity makes their investment valuation materially more demanding than conventional generation.

This client held a percentage share in an ongoing hydro pump storage capacity expansion project. When project costs began exceeding initial estimates, management needed a structured, defensible basis for deciding whether to increase the investment, maintain the current share, or exit — within a compressed decision timeline.

"Investment decisions in hydro pump storage require scenario-based thinking — the asset's multi-market value structure means a single-point valuation will always be misleading."

Crescenti Pohl Advisory · Project observation

Client situation

Project context
  • Hydro pump storage capacity expansion ongoing, with the client holding a percentage share
  • Project capital costs had exceeded initial estimates, creating a decision point
  • Asset value dependent on multiple market vectors — energy, ancillary services, and capacity
The decision required
  • Structured basis for a binary decision: increase investment, hold current share, or exit
  • Valuation needed to reflect the full multi-market value of the asset, not a simplified IRR
  • Decision framework required clear thresholds so management could act decisively

Our contribution

Three-scenario market framework

Established bear, base and bull scenarios for the energy and ancillary power markets relevant to the asset — providing a range of valuations that reflected genuine market uncertainty rather than false precision.

Asset valuation across energy and ancillary markets

Valued the hydro pump storage asset under each scenario, integrating revenue streams from energy arbitrage, frequency response, capacity market participation and balancing services into a single consolidated valuation model.

Options valuation and cost sensitivity analysis

Applied options valuation methodology to quantify the value of flexibility — specifically the value of maintaining the ability to increase or exit the investment — given the range of cost outcomes and market scenarios.

Structured (des-)investment decision framework

Translated the valuation analysis into a clear decision framework with explicit thresholds — defining the project cost and market conditions under which management should exit, reduce, hold, or increase the investment share.

Outcome

The client was able to take a structured, board-ready investment decision supported by a transparent scenario framework and a clear articulation of the value at risk under each outcome.

The decision framework — with its explicit exit, hold and increase thresholds — remained a reference tool through subsequent project cost reviews, allowing management to respond quickly to new information without re-commissioning a full analytical process.
